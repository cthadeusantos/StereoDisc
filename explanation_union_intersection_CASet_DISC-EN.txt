CASet and DISC: comparison by intersection and by union
=======================================================

This file explains the variants implemented in CASet.py and DISC.py, based
on the article "Distance measures for tumor evolutionary trees," by DiNardo
et al. (Bioinformatics, 2020).

1. What the metrics compare
---------------------------

A tumor evolutionary tree has mutations at its nodes. For each mutation i,
consider A(i) as the set of mutations ancestral to i.

For a pair of mutations i and j:

- C(i,j) is the set of mutations common to the ancestors of i and j.
- D(i,j) = A(i) - A(j) is the set of mutations ancestral to i that are not
  ancestral to j.

CASet computes the average of the Jaccard distances between the sets C(i,j)
of the two trees, for unordered pairs of mutations.

DISC computes the average of the Jaccard distances between the sets D(i,j)
of the two trees, for distinct ordered pairs (i,j). DISC considers the
direction of the ancestral relationship, which is why it compares (i,j) and
(j,i).

The Jaccard distance between X and Y is:

    1 - |X intersection Y| / |X union Y|

If X and Y are both empty, the article defines the Jaccard distance as 0.

2. Intersection of mutations (-- default)
-----------------------------------------

If M1 and M2 are the sets of mutation labels of the two trees, the
intersection is:

    I = M1 intersection M2

The metrics consider only pairs formed by mutations in I:

- CASet compares C1(i,j) and C2(i,j) for each unordered pair in I.
- DISC compares D1(i,j) and D2(i,j) for each distinct ordered pair in I.

This variant focuses the comparison on shared mutations and reduces the
effect of differences in labels present in only one tree. It is useful, for
example, when one wants to compare evolutionary patterns between trees that
do not contain exactly the same mutations.

Important: the article notes that the compared ancestor sets C(i,j) and
D(i,j) may still contain mutations present in only one of the trees.
Therefore, the intersection variant is not equivalent to deleting all
exclusive mutations, contracting the tree, and then comparing the resulting
topologies.

In the program, intersection is the default. Not passing -u selects CASet∩
or DISC∩.

3. Union of mutations (-u)
--------------------------

The union is:

    U = M1 union M2

The metrics consider pairs formed from the entire set U. When a mutation
does not exist in one of the trees, the article defines its set of ancestors
in that tree as empty. Thus, differences in the presence or absence of
mutations can also contribute to the distance.

This variant is useful when the difference between the sets of labels should
be considered, as in comparisons between trees inferred from different data
types, different collection times, or different samples.

In the program, passing -u selects CASet∪ or DISC∪. The option affects both
the pairwise comparisons and the comparison of the consensus tree with each
tree.

4. How to interpret values and edge cases
-----------------------------------------

The outputs are distances, not similarity scores: 0 means that the sets
compared by the chosen variant coincided; higher values indicate greater
difference in the considered sets. A zero in the intersection variant does
not guarantee that the complete trees are identical, since exclusive
mutations may have been ignored.

The formulas require at least two mutations in the set used to form pairs.
If there are fewer than two, there are no pairs to compute the average. In
this program, the result is NaN (undefined), appears in gray in the plot,
and is saved as NaN in the NumPy file. The article explicitly describes the
case of disjoint mutation sets for the intersection variant as a degenerate
case with distance 0; the program marks any case with fewer than two
mutations as undefined, so as not to confuse the absence of pairs with a
computed distance equal to zero.

5. Mathematical properties
--------------------------

The article demonstrates CASet and DISC as metrics when the compared trees
have the same set of mutations. For the union and intersection extensions,
the supplement shows that the triangle inequality can fail in general.
Moreover, the intersection variant can produce distance zero between
distinct trees, for example when they do not share labels. Therefore, in the
variants with different sets, "distance" names the score defined by the
formulas, but it does not necessarily satisfy all the mathematical
properties of a metric.
